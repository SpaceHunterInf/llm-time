TL;DR

  1. We asked whether an LLM selectively revises linked predictions and leaves unrelated ones alone, given new evidence.
  2. With thinking, frozen Gemini solves the one-event numeric task when structure is supplied. Carrying its own previous forecast forward is much harder than reconstructing from the tape.
  3. We retired a proposed paired loss and trained an ordinary posterior-KL baseline (tiny MLP, linear head, LoRA) on a 16-state filter.
  4. LoRA beats the other learners, but every learner loses to cheap statistical filters — even on training data. Longer MLP training helps, still far from the independent filter.

The question after Week 2

Week 2: a frozen LLM forms a hypothesis, doubts, and often resurrects the old one. Recency prompts help on change and hurt on no-change. That is not a general revision loop.

This fortnight we isolated a cleaner computational demand:

Use new evidence to update linked mechanisms, preserve unrelated forecasts, and only then choose a costly diagnosis.

Four components, known reset groups, known previous joint, short suffix of observations. Exact Bayes is available as a reference, not as the proposed paper.

flowchart TB A[Evidence about some components] --> B{Shared reset group?} B -->|yes| C[Revise linked unobserved too] B -->|no| D[Leave unrelated forecasts alone] C --> E[Decide: exploit / diagnose / safe] D --> E

Three qualifications that matter in the meeting:
  • Head ≈ MLP. Frozen Qwen text does not beat a 47-d numeric encoder. This task does not justify an “LLM method” claim.
  • ~78% of LoRA’s structural gain vs head is on h=0, where grouping has no dynamical effect. Remaining h=.03 gap is small.
  • Almost no decision headroom over independence: structural regret 0.00075 (independent) vs 0.0086 (LoRA). Fixing the last coupling would not unlock a large average utility story on this distribution.

Wall: ~2 hours on a Quadro RTX 8000. All nine best checkpoints are the last step — the 3-epoch recipe had not converged (and its LR hits 0 at step 192).

75 seconds, $0. Longer ordinary KL helped this net. It does not say Qwen would get the same lift, and it does not make approximating a known 16-state filter a paper.

What we do not claim

  • No novel learner. Ordinary distillation of a supplied posterior.
  • No evidence that language is necessary here. MLP matches the LM head; independent filtering wins.
  • No demonstration of discovering structure, or of long-horizon learned memory.
  • MemoPilot already trains a memory updater for a frozen player. WorldEvolver already revises deployment-time context. Generic “learned memory” is occupied.

What we do have: an exact reference, sealed-ish splits, cheap statistical controls, checked decision scoring, a resumable tiny training path, and several easy stories we can stop telling.

Ask for the group

Recommendation already taken internally: close the automatic numeric-training queue. Do not buy another Qwen run to chase the independent filter on this supplied-structure task.

The original scientific bet is still worth a design discussion, not more of the same benchmark:

Infer which shared mechanism failed from language and experience, decide how far a correction should propagate, and show transfer to an unobserved linked task without harming an unrelated one — against generic memory revision and against a statistical observer that is allowed to infer dependencies.

A later candidate (not run): limited effort among exploiting the current strategy, checking whether its evidence went stale, and proposing/validating a repair under hidden drift. That would need a discriminating example and a literature case, not another 16-state KL table.

Raw reports: one-event live, sequential v2, state handoff, paired-loss feasibility, GPU pilot, 30-epoch MLP diagnosis. Spend cap throughout: ~$20/run; Gemini not DeepSeek.
